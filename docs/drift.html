Drift in the Fact Store

A hypothesis · An evaluation · A repair loop

You kept the facts.
The order moved.

Distil a document into atomic facts and store them, and you keep the words. The hypothesis behind this page is that what you lose is the connective tissue: the conjunctions, the conditions, and the order that says what to have ready first. The questions were whether an evaluation can see that loss where atom coverage can't, whether, once seen, it can be sent back to be rebuilt, and, after a first round of runs, whether the extractor had been answering the question before the round trip could.

It had. The first extractor kept half the conditions inside the atoms, and under it two short passages came through the round trip almost untouched. An extractor that is forbidden to do that produces the loss the hypothesis predicted: the regeneration gets half the multi-fact questions wrong on both passages, and on ten how-to pages it springs half the prerequisites on the reader. Fed back what the evaluation found missing, the regeneration recovers most of the order and most of the bridges. Twenty more pages, chosen by rule, say the same, and add that a third of them came back as something other than a how-to.

01 · The hypothesis, on one paragraph

Three facts that only matter together, and an extractor that kept them that way.

A single paragraph about Lambda logging. An LLM extracts the facts. It then writes new documentation from those facts alone, with the edges withheld: that is the round trip a fact store imposes. Walk through what happened at each stage under the first extractor, in the run closest to the median of five.

The source passage. Conjunctive structure highlighted.

The CloudWatch log group is created automatically, but only when all three conditions hold: the Lambda execution role has logs:CreateLogGroup and logs:PutLogEvents permissions, and the function completes at least one successful invocation.

Without all three, the function appears to run successfully, but no logs are written and no error is surfaced.

The phrase "only when all three conditions hold" encodes a conjunction. The hypothesis says atomization strips it: the conjunction lives between the facts, and a flat store has no field for it. Whether that happens depends on where the extractor puts it.

Six atoms, where a clean atomization would give five. The extractor kept the conjunction inside f01: "created automatically only when all three conditions hold." It also recorded the conjunction as a REQUIRES edge, and the store drops the edge. On this passage, under this extractor, the hypothesis cannot fail: the sentence that held the meaning was kept whole as an atom.

Regenerated from the six atoms only. Conjunction kept. Added.

The CloudWatch log group for a Lambda function is created automatically only when all three of the following conditions hold. First, the Lambda execution role must have the logs:CreateLogGroup permission. Second, the execution role must also have the logs:PutLogEvents permission. Third, the function must complete at least one successful invocation.

If any of these conditions is not met, the function appears to run successfully, but no logs are written. No error is surfaced in this case, so the missing log group can go unnoticed unless you check for it explicitly.

The conjunction survived, in all five runs. A developer reading this adds all three permissions. What the regeneration added is the closing advice, which no atom supports. The probes, which read the text, got 24 of 25 right. The graph bridge score is noisier, 0% in three runs, because the re-extraction phrased an antecedent far enough from the original atom to miss the match; a reader asked whether the text states each edge finds the REQUIRES edge in all five drafts and the SEQUENCE edge in none, 63% every time.

Repaired: regenerated again with the two relationships the evaluation reported lost, stated in words. Rebuilt.

Second, the execution role has the logs:PutLogEvents permission. Third, the function completes at least one successful invocation. The repaired draft states the SEQUENCE edge the first draft dropped. The text reader finds both edges in all five repaired drafts, 100%; the graph matcher finds them in three, and in the other two the repaired draft re-split its sentences and lost the match it had. On six atoms the graph score is a coin flip; the text reader and the probes are the steadier instruments.

The same passage under a strict extractor. Forbid conditions inside atoms and the passage becomes seven unconditional claims: "The CloudWatch log group is created automatically", "The Lambda execution role has the logs:CreateLogGroup permission", and so on, with one REQUIRES edge carrying the conjunction and one IMPLIES edge. Regenerated from those atoms alone, the drafts still often say "only if", but the probes now get 12 of 25 right against 24 of 25, and drift is 42%. That is the hypothesis holding where the first extractor had hidden it. Section 02 says how much hiding there was.

02 · The extractor's thumb on the scale

Half the conditions stayed inside the atoms.

The first extraction prompt asked for "one complete, self-contained sentence" per fact and told the model to "preserve negations, conditions, quantifiers, and scope word-for-word." It did. Across every source run under it, 49% of the atoms carry a conditional word: "only when", "if", "unless", "must", "before". The extractor was storing half the connective tissue inside the facts, and the round trip was being asked how much of that the regeneration keeps. That is a real question about regeneration, and a different one from the hypothesis.

The strict extractor forbids conditions inside atoms: when the source says A holds only when B and C, it writes A, B and C as three facts and the dependence as an edge. It is the extractor a fact store under test should have. On the two passages it folds nothing. On the pages it still folds between a tenth and a half of the atoms, because a step like "you must create the bucket before you enable logging" resists being split, so the two extractors differ in degree on pages and in kind on passages.

03 · A passage built to lose

The condition arrives late. Then it reverses.

The Lambda passage states its condition up front, in one sentence, which is the easiest case for an extractor to keep whole. This one does what documentation does more often: it states a rule, qualifies it two sentences later, and overrides the qualification with an account-level setting after that. Five runs under each extractor.

The source passage. The rule, its qualification, and the override.

New buckets block public access by default, so objects in them cannot be read anonymously even when a bucket policy grants s3:GetObject to everyone. Attaching such a policy succeeds without any error.

If you turn off the bucket's Block Public Access settings, the policy takes effect and the objects become readable by anyone. However, if Block Public Access is also enabled at the account level, the account setting overrides the bucket setting, and the objects stay private no matter what the bucket policy says.

Enabling static website hosting on the bucket does not change any of this: the website endpoint returns 403 until both the account-level and the bucket-level settings allow public access. Unless you check the account setting first, you can spend a long time editing a policy that is being ignored.

Under the sentence extractor this passage becomes nine atoms, six of them carrying their own condition, and the round trip keeps 71 to 87% of the bridges and 24 of 25 probes. Under the strict extractor it becomes sixteen unconditional atoms and nine edges.

This draft reads well and reassembles most of the logic; it still answered two of five probes wrong, and across five runs the probes came out 13 of 25 against 24 of 25 under the sentence extractor, with drift 43% against 9%. Repaired, the probes rose to 17 of 25 and drift fell in four runs of five. The regeneration is good at prose and unreliable at logic it was not handed, which is the whole claim.

04 · The conditional graph

Edges carry the logic atoms can't.

The library extracts five types of relationship between facts: requires, implies, contradicts, sequence, and negates. Each type names a failure mode that flattening can cause, and each is what the repair pass feeds back when the evaluation reports it lost.

Requires

The consequent is true only when all listed antecedents hold at once. Lost most often: a conjunction becomes three independent conditions. The developer satisfies one and considers themselves done.

Implies

One fact alone is sufficient for another. Lost most often in causal chains: "A causes B causes C" becomes three unconnected claims. No one knows B is the cause.

Contradicts and negates

Two facts cannot both hold, or one denies the other. Regenerations often state both as conditions without saying they exclude each other, and the result is a setup nobody can build.

Sequence

B presupposes A, so order matters. Regenerated documents reorder steps because atoms carry no position. The reader tries step 3 before step 1 is done.

Left: the strict extractor's graph of the Lambda passage, with the conjunction as an edge. Right: what the atom store keeps. Under the sentence extractor f01 would read "created automatically only when all three conditions hold" and the dropped edge would cost nothing; under the strict one f01 is unconditional and the edge is the only place the condition lived.

05 · The evaluation

Four layers, one composite.

Each metric catches a different failure mode. You need all of them because a document can pass one test and fail another, as every source here did. Each also reports what it could not find, which is what the repair pass feeds back, and the report now prints two numbers about the extraction itself beside them.

1

Atomic F1 — did the content survive?

The library embeds every fact from the original and the regenerated document. Recall is the share of original atoms that claim a regenerated atom in a one-to-one assignment at or above 0.70 cosine, highest similarity first. Precision is the share of regenerated atoms within 0.70 of any original, so a fact the regeneration split in two is not two hallucinations. The unsupported atoms are listed. Weight in composite: 0.25. Treat it as a floor: a regeneration needs high F1, and high F1 says nothing about structure.

2

Conditional bridge recall — did the structure survive?

Using the same assignment, the library checks, for each original conditional edge, whether the regen graph has an edge of the same kind whose antecedents are exactly the mapped ones and whose consequent either is the mapped atom or is within 0.70 of the original consequent. Each edge counts by its extraction confidence, and the lost edges are returned by index. Weight in composite: 0.50, double the others, because atomic F1 cannot see this failure at all. The re-extraction is the noisy step, so --repeats reports the range.

2b

Text bridge recall — does the text state the relationship?

The same original edges, rendered as sentences in the atoms' own words ("X holds only when all of the following hold: A; B; C"), put to a model reading the regenerated document in one call, with a supporting quote asked for. The confidence-weighted share of yes answers is text bridge recall; an "unknown" counts as lost. It asks about the text rather than a second extraction of it, so it does not inherit the re-extraction's noise. Across the recorded runs it agrees with the graph measure moderately under the strict extractor and not at all under the sentence one, and departs from it most where the re-extraction fails to find edges the text plainly states. It is the measure the repair comparison uses; the composite still carries the graph measure.

3

Entailment probes — does the regeneration behave correctly?

The library writes yes/no questions that take several facts to answer, aimed at REQUIRES edges with two or more antecedents, and puts each one to the regenerated document. The pass rate measures behaviour: whether the conjunctive structure disappeared from the reader's experience, even where the matchers say otherwise. An "unknown" counts as wrong. Weight in composite: 0.25. Under the strict extractor the probes are harder: with the logic in the edges, the generator writes contrapositives, "if the log group was not created and the role has both permissions, must the function have failed to run?", and those are the questions a reader of a flattened document gets wrong.

4

Structural drift — did the prerequisite ordering survive?

For procedure pages, the library runs the prerequisite extractor from the detour work on both documents and compares them: how many prerequisites survived, matched by embedding at the same 0.70, and how much more detour-prone the regeneration became. The loss is 0.6 times the recall loss plus 0.4 times the detour-rate delta; if the regeneration no longer reads as a procedure, the loss is 1. Whether the original is a procedure is a vote over three reads. The unmatched prerequisites and the ones that arrive as detours are returned by name. Weight in composite: 0.20, added when applicable.

+

Granularity and load — what the extractor did, and how much logic there was

Granularity is atoms per sentence and the share of atoms that carry a conditional word: the measure of how much the extractor kept inside the facts. Conditional load is how many ifs, ands and buts the source asks a reader to hold at once: condition words per sentence, edges per atom, the widest REQUIRES junction and the longest chain of dependences, scaled to one number from 0 for a list of independent facts to 1 for dense nested logic. Neither enters the composite. Both are printed beside it, because a drift score without them is a score of an unknown test.

09 · How many ifs, ands and buts

Harder passages drift about the same.

Some passages you read five times and still aren't sure you have them. The conditional load number is an attempt to measure that from the text and its graph: condition words per sentence, edges per atom, the widest REQUIRES junction, the longest chain. The contravention passage scores 0.56, the Lambda passage 0.44, the pages 0.34 to 0.66. If the hypothesis is right, harder passages should lose more in the round trip. One point per source, drift on the vertical axis.

Strict extractor. Amber: the passages and the two single pages; violet: the cohort pages. Rank correlation of load with drift −0.09 on 33 sources, bootstrap interval −0.43 to +0.27; with bridge loss +0.07, with structural loss −0.23.

No relation the data can support. Under the strict extractor the rank correlation between load and drift is −0.09 on thirteen sources, with a bootstrap interval from −0.43 to +0.27; against bridge loss and structural loss the intervals are as wide. Under the sentence extractor it comes out −0.14, which is the extractor folding the harder sentences into atoms, where they are safe. Two readings follow. The first is that thirteen or fourteen points cannot find a moderate effect, which is true and is what more pages are for. The second is that what the round trip loses is governed by what the extractor externalised, and the load number measures the source, so it predicts loss only through an extractor that respects it. The difficulty you feel reading a passage is real; it is not yet a quantity this evaluation can use. A reader-side measure, the probes put to the original rather than the regeneration, would be the next thing to try.

10 · The repair loop

Score it, name what's missing, send it back.

The evaluation returns more than numbers. Bridge recall returns the edges it could not find. The structural layer returns the prerequisites it could not match and the ones that arrive as detours. The repair pass hands those to the regenerator with the same atoms: each lost relationship stated in words ("[f01] holds only when [f02] and [f03] and [f04] all hold"), and the prerequisites to state up front, with where their steps are. Then it scores the new draft with the same four layers. Two more model calls per page, and the loop can run again.

What it fixes: the order

Under either extractor. On the ten pages, prerequisite recall rose from a median of 72% to 89% and structural loss halved; the detour rate after the round trip fell on five pages, and the one page that had come back as prose came back as a procedure. On the CloudWatch page the sprung prerequisites went to zero in every run. Telling the regenerator what to put first works.

What it fixes, read from the text: the bridges

Measured by a reader of the regenerated text, feeding the lost edges back raises bridge recall on 8 of 10 pages under the strict extractor, median 69% to 89%, and on 10 of 10 under the sentence extractor. On the CloudWatch page the reader finds 57% of the edges stated in the first draft and 93% in the repaired one; the graph matcher had read the same pair as 9% and 30%. The earlier conclusion that repair could not move the bridges was the matcher's noise, not the draft's. The passage's probes moved too, from 13 to 17 of 25 under the strict extractor.

The loop as it stands is a proof of the shape: a round trip that loses structure, an evaluation that names the loss, a regeneration that takes the names back, and now a reader that can tell a repaired bridge from a re-split one. Atom F1 drops a few points after repair, because the repaired draft restructures and the re-extraction splits it differently; the probes do not. What it needs next is more pages.

Which instrument to trust. Across every scored draft and repair under the strict extractor, the graph matcher and the text reader agree moderately, rank correlation +0.42; under the sentence extractor they do not agree at all, +0.05, because the matcher is scoring how the re-extraction split sentences that already carried their conditions. The text reader agrees with the probes only loosely, +0.09, and that is expected: the probes ask contrapositive questions a document can entail without stating. Where the three disagree, read the quotes the reader returns.

11 · Running it

One script. One report.

The pipeline is a single script in experiments/doc_drift/. Pass a URL or a local file. It fetches, extracts, regenerates, re-extracts, and scores; with --repair it regenerates once more with what was lost and scores that too. It needs a Claude model for extraction and an OpenAI key for embeddings; --mock runs offline with a hash embedder and tells you nothing about meaning.

python -I experiments/doc_drift/run.py <url-or-file>
python -I experiments/doc_drift/run.py <url> --atomizer strict   # no conditions inside atoms; the test the hypothesis needs
python -I experiments/doc_drift/run.py <url> --repair            # feed back the lost edges and sprung prerequisites

Reading the report

Look at the granularity line first: if half the atoms carry a condition, the bridge and probe scores are about regeneration, not about the store. Atomic loss near zero with a positive detour delta means the how-to kept its facts and moved its prerequisites; the repair line says whether they went back. Low precision on a short passage with the probes passing means the regeneration added advice the source never gave. Bridge recall that swings between runs means the re-extraction is the noisy part; use --repeats before quoting it.

How sure

Ten cohort pages at two runs per extractor, three on the CloudWatch page, five on each passage; one model for extraction (Claude Sonnet 5.5) and one embedding model. The original graph is extracted once per extractor and cached, so the variation between runs comes from the regeneration and its re-extraction. The 0.70 threshold was set by looking at the Lambda runs, so it is calibrated on data it also scores. The cohort pages were chosen because three reads agreed they are procedures, which selects for clear how-tos. The strict extractor still folds between a tenth and a half of the atoms on pages, so the "strict" rows are stricter, not pure. The repair fed back only what the graph matcher flagged; feeding back every edge was tried on two sources and did no better, and feeding back what the text reader flagged is now an option that has not been run. The text reader is one model's judgement of one call per draft, with unknowns counted as lost; on long pages it answers unknown often. The holdout was drawn by rule and run once, so its rates are a check on the cohort's, not a second experiment. Thirty pages is a rate, not a law.

Using the Python API

Import the metrics from semantic_ruler.drift as pure functions: atomic_f1, cond_bridge_recall, entailment_rate, structural_drift, drift_score, granularity, conditional_load, bridge_entailment for the text reader, and summarize_repeats for the spread over runs. extract_facts takes atomizer="strict"; regenerate_doc takes repair_edges and prerequisites for the repair pass. The shared shard store caches extracted graphs, so you pay the LLM extraction cost once per document and extractor.
